1. Purpose and Regulatory Alignment
These instructions establish the minimum mandatory preparation standards for students
appearing in EduQual Level 3, 4, 5, and 6 assessments.
The objective is to ensure that:
● Learning outcomes are fully met and demonstrable
● Assessments align with EduQual and Ofqual expectations
● Submissions reflect industry readiness and employer-aligned competence
● Academic integrity is preserved while responsibly leveraging AI as a tool
This framework reflects:
● EduQual assessment principles
● Ofqual conditions of recognition
● EduQual & RQF level descriptors
● Current global industry practices
2. Mandatory Preparation Requirements (Non-Negotiable)
Every student MUST ensure the following are fully prepared for each selected topic, where
applicable.
2.1 Conceptual and Theoretical Readiness
Students must demonstrate:
● Clear understanding of core concepts, terminology, and frameworks
● Ability to explain why a solution exists, not just what it is

===== PAGE 2 =====
● Alignment of explanations with the RQF level depth (increasing complexity from Level 3
to Level 6)
Superficial explanations or memorized definitions are insufficient.
2.2 Design & Architecture Artifacts (Mandatory Where Applicable)
For every topic, students MUST be able to independently explain and defend the following
artifacts:
● System Architecture Diagrams
● Network Diagrams
● Data Flow Diagrams (DFDs)
● Process Flow / Workflow Diagrams
● Security Architecture Diagrams
● Cloud / Hybrid / On-Prem Architecture Designs
Requirements:
● Diagrams must be logically consistent
● Components must be correctly placed and justified
● Data movement, trust boundaries, and dependencies must be clearly explained
● The student must be able to modify or redesign the architecture live during Q&A
Pre-made diagrams without understanding will be penalized.
2.3 Implementation & Demonstration Capability
Students must be able to demonstrate:
● How the solution works end-to-end
● How components interact in real scenarios
● How the design would be implemented in practice
This includes:
● Configuration logic
● Deployment flow
● Operational behavior
● Failure and recovery scenarios
● Scalability and security considerations
Live demos, walkthroughs, or simulated demonstrations may be required depending on the
topic and level.

2.4 Policies, Procedures, and Governance (Where Applicable)
Students must prepare and be able to justify:
● Policies (what must be followed and why)
● Procedures (how things are executed step-by-step)
● Standards and controls aligned with industry frameworks
● Compliance, risk, and governance considerations
At higher RQF levels (5–6), students must show:
● Decision-making rationale
● Trade-off analysis
● Alignment with regulatory and organizational objectives
3. Use of Artificial Intelligence (AI): Allowed and Restricted Areas
3.1 Permitted Use of AI (Explicitly Allowed)
AI may be used for:
● Writing code
● Generating scripts
● Producing configuration samples
● Assisting with documentation drafts
● Demonstrating how a solution operates
This reflects modern industry reality where AI-assisted development is standard practice.
3.2 Restricted Use of AI (Strictly Evaluated)
AI MUST NOT replace:
● Student understanding
● Student reasoning
● Student design thinking
● Student explanation
● Student problem-solving during Q&A
All of the following are individually evaluated:
● Creativity

● Logical flow
● Technical justification
● Real-world reasoning
● Ability to defend decisions verbally
AI usage is assessed as a tool, not as a substitute for competence.

4.2 Q&A / Interview Evaluation (50% | Total Marks: 25)
Students must demonstrate:
● Real-time reasoning
● Design defense
● Scenario handling
● Employer-style questioning readiness
A minimum of 10 structured questions may be asked, covering:
● Architecture decisions
● Failure scenarios
● Security implications
● Performance trade-offs
● Real-world deployment challenges
5. Performance Expectations by RQF Level
● RQF Level 3: Foundational understanding and basic demonstrations
● RQF Level 4: Applied knowledge with structured designs
● RQF Level 5: Analytical thinking, optimization, and justification
● RQF Level 6: Strategic architecture, risk-based decisions, and enterprise alignment
Expectations increase significantly with each level.

6. Final Verdict and Recommendations
Final outcomes include:
● Strengths
● Areas needing attention
● Clear recommendation
● Final score out of 50
● Performance level classification
Passing requires both technical competence and demonstrable understanding.
7. Institutional Position
Al Nafi International College evaluates:
● Skills, not templates
● Understanding, not memorization
● Readiness for employment, not academic theory alone
Students are expected to perform as entry-level to mid-level professionals, depending on the
RQF level.
8. Closing Statement
This instruction set exists to:
● Protect qualification integrity
● Align education with real-world demand
● Ensure students graduate with defensible, employable skills
Students are encouraged to prepare thoroughly, think critically, and present confidently.
The assessment process is rigorous by design and fair by principle.
